# Buyer walkthrough: four prospective clients on dev.base-xtech.com

Date: 2026-09-24
Method: I fetched every page listed in the brief with curl on 2026-09-24 and read the rendered text, including headings, FAQ questions, stage labels, card titles, the contact drawer and the footer. I also read the real blog (base-xtech.com/blog/) and the UK gifting case study. Each persona is a buyer with a real trigger who is comparing 3–4 agencies. Quotes are verbatim site text. Where I infer something (for example, where a button leads), I say so.

Scope note: the home page still carries the old copy and there is no About page yet. I only count those against the site where a persona actually gets sent there (for example the "Team" nav link).

## Persona 1: Katrin, Head of E-Commerce, German mid-size retailer

**Profile:** EUR 8–15M revenue, 3 stores plus Shopify, Xentral ERP. Stock mismatches between POS and online. Searched "Shopify Xentral Anbindung" and landed on `/de/pages/complex-solutions/`. She is also talking to two German Shopify Plus partners and one Xentral-certified integrator.

### First 10 seconds

- **H1:** "Komplexe Systeme, so verbunden, wie Ihr Unternehmen arbeitet." Then the system strip with **"Xentral"** second in the list.
- **What she gets:** these people connect Shopify to ERPs like mine. Xentral is named, along with JTL, weclapp, DATEV and Lexware. That is the DACH vocabulary, and it tells her the page was written for her. Good hook.
- **What she doesn't get:**
  - Who "wir" is.
  - Where they sit.
  - Whether Xentral is something they *have done* or only *could do*.
- The line "Integrierbar (noch kein abgeschlossenes Projekt): SAP Business One · Akeneo · Salesforce" is unusually honest. By contrast it implies Xentral **has** a delivered project. She registers that and wants to see it.

### Section by section

| Section | Question in her head | Answered? |
|---|---|---|
| System strip | "Have you actually done Xentral, and which direction? Stock from Xentral to Shopify, orders back?" | **Implied, never shown.** No Xentral case, no Xentral detail anywhere on the page. |
| "Expertise vor Ort", Alexander Karl, DACH-Partner | "Vor Ort. So there's someone in Germany I can meet? Who is he, is he employed or a reseller, where is he?" | **No.** The photo is a grey placeholder; its accessible label literally reads "Foto von Alexander Karl (Platzhalter)". No LinkedIn, no city, no email. The quote is generic ("nicht nur darum, APIs zu verbinden"). A named partner without a face reads worse than no partner at all. |
| "Unsere Leistungen" (ERP, CRM, APIs, private apps, automation, logistics) | "Fine, standard." | Yes, but it's undifferentiated. |
| "Unter der Haube / Was eine Integration zuverlässig macht" ("Feldzuordnung und Datenhoheit… Webhooks und API-Limits… Wiederholungsversuche, Datenabgleich und Monitoring") | "Do they know what they're doing technically?" | **Yes, and this is the best block for her.** It uses the words her IT lead would use. |
| "Systemarchitektur" | "Show me the diagram with Xentral, POS, Shopify." | Only a sentence of copy in text form. Whatever visual exists, it's generic, not her stack. |
| "Wann wir helfen können": "Ihre Systeme laufen auseinander… Inkonsistenzen, Verzögerungen und Handarbeit" | "That's me." | **Yes, pain named exactly.** |
| Fresh Market case | "Is this like us? Which POS, which ERP, how many locations, what sync interval, what was the oversell rate before and after?" | **Partly.** The problem is her problem ("ein im Laden verkaufter Artikel online weiterhin verfügbar"). But there are no numbers, no country, no systems named (is it Shopify POS? Xentral? neither?), no logo and no quote. "Key outcomes" are capability labels restated ("Bestandsabgleich: Die Verfügbarkeit bleibt… synchron"), not outcomes. |
| "Tiefer einsteigen" blog cards | "Anything about Xentral / German retail?" | **No.** The cards are a UK gifting brand, *Nova Poshta* ("Ukrainische Shopify-Shops mussten Nova-Poshta-Frachtbriefe…"), and an AI analytics agent built "mit der Claude-API". German titles link to English articles. Nova Poshta is the moment she realises this is a Ukrainian agency, and she learns it from a blog teaser, not from the company telling her. |
| Phase 1–4 ("System erfassen… Livegang & Monitoring") | "Fixed scope or T&M? What do I get at the end of phase 1: a Pflichtenheft/Lastenheft? Is phase 1 paid separately?" | **No.** The stages describe activities, not deliverables, duration or commercial model. |
| FAQ | "Wer betreut die Integration nach dem Livegang?" / "Wie verhindern Sie, dass Bestände auseinanderlaufen?" | **Good answers.** "Indem wir festlegen, welches System für den Bestand führend ist" is exactly the right answer. But: "Mit welchen ERP- und CRM-Systemen haben Sie bereits gearbeitet?" answers with "Mit ERP-, CRM-, PIM- und Fulfillment-Systemen aus den gängigen Kategorien". She asked *which*, and the answer dodges. After the logo strip, this reads as evasive. |
| Timeline FAQ | "How long for Xentral + POS?" | Only "Eine einzelne, gut dokumentierte Anbindung ist eine Frage von Wochen" and the UK case at "vier bis sechs Monate". No price anchor. |

### Pains named vs missed

- **Named:** inventory drift, manual re-keying, no fitting app, monitoring after go-live.
- **Missed (her real checklist):**
  - DSGVO / AVV (Auftragsverarbeitungsvertrag). Her customer and order data will pass through their middleware. Where is it hosted? EU?
  - Contract under German law, invoicing in EUR, a legal entity she can put in her vendor system.
  - Warranty and SLA (Reaktionszeiten, Wartungsvertrag).
  - Fixed-price option after discovery.
  - Xentral-specific depth: Xentral API limits, Lagerplätze, Chargen/MHD, multiple Lager ↔ Shopify Locations, returns.
  - Handling during the Christmas peak.
  - Business continuity for a team in Ukraine (she won't ask this out loud, but her CFO will).
  - A German-speaking contact on calls, not just on the page.

### Trust and distrust

- **Trust:** the honest "noch kein abgeschlossenes Projekt" line; the DACH system list; "Datenhoheit" language; the FAQ on which system is authoritative.
- **Distrust, in order of damage:**
  1. **No Impressum.** For a German B2B buyer this is disqualifying on its own. It signals "not a real company in the German sense" and she will stop evaluating seriously.
  2. **Placeholder photo for the "Expertise vor Ort" partner.** "Vor Ort" promises local presence; the page can't show his face.
  3. **Everything around the German content is English:** nav, "let's discuss a project", and the success message "We will back shortly to you with response." The broken English in the success message is the last thing she sees, and it undermines the precision the page promised.
  4. Budget in **$**, with a top bucket of **"$25k+"** for a mid-size retailer whose project is likely EUR 30–80k.
  5. Ukraine is discovered indirectly (Nova Poshta), not stated. That feels like concealment, even though it isn't meant to be.
  6. Privacy policy: "By using our Website, you agree…" is not how consent works under DSGVO. There is no entity or address, and governing law is Ukraine.

### Drop-off

She scrolls to Fresh Market, finds no numbers and no Xentral, clicks a blog card and lands on Nova Poshta or an English article. Then she looks for the Impressum in the footer to find out who they are, and it doesn't exist. **She drops off there.** If she is very pained, she might first open the drawer, see $ and English, and close it.

### Form?

**No** (maybe, if a colleague referred her). If she did write:

> "Wir nutzen Xentral mit 3 Filialen (Shopify POS?) und Shopify online, ca. 40k SKUs. Bestände laufen auseinander. Haben Sie Xentral bereits angebunden? Bitte um Referenz und Angaben zu DSGVO/AVV, Vertragspartner und Festpreis für die Konzeptphase."

- **Annoying fields:**
  - Budget in USD with "$25k+" as the ceiling.
  - Required phone. She would rather be emailed first.
  - "Service required" has no "ERP/POS integration" option. "API Integration" is the nearest.
  - The whole form is in English on a German page.

### What she'd need to move forward

- An Impressum with a legal entity (ideally a German/EU contracting entity or a named German partner company).
- A named Xentral reference with numbers ("3 Filialen, Bestandsdifferenzen von X auf Y, Sync-Intervall Z Minuten").
- Alexander Karl with photo, LinkedIn and "spricht Deutsch, erreichbar unter…".
- One line on DSGVO/AVV and EU hosting.
- A paid discovery phase with a fixed price and a defined deliverable.
- A German form in EUR.

### Versus a strong German competitor

A German Plus partner shows:

- Impressum and GmbH
- team photos in an office in Hamburg/Munich
- Xentral partner badge
- 3 named Mittelstand references with logos and a quote from a Geschäftsführer
- "Kostenloses Erstgespräch" via a calendar
- AVV template on request
- day rates or "Projekte ab 25.000 €"

Base X's copy is technically sharper than most of them, but it loses on every trust signal she actually checks.

  - Required phone: fine for him.

- A configurator case study with a before/after metric, the build time, and the stack (Liquid + app block? Checkout Extensibility? metaobjects?).

- The name and face of the lead developer.
- A price anchor that matches the H1 ("Custom builds with configurator or B2B typically £15–40k").
- Payment milestones and code ownership.
- Remove the Ukraine line and the template package copy from the pricing card.

### Versus a strong competitor

A good UK Shopify Plus agency shows:

- Shopify Plus Partner badge
- 5–8 named case studies with metrics
- founder and dev team photos
- "Projects typically start from £20k"
- a calendar booking

Base X's copy beats them for credibility of *thinking*. Its pricing card makes Base X look like a cheaper, riskier option than it actually is.

## Persona 3: Marta, operations manager, EU brand migrating to Shopify

**Profile:** An EU brand on Magento 1 (EOL since 2020) with a WooCommerce satellite store. 10 years of customer and order data. Terrified of losing SEO rankings and orders during the switch. Landed on `/pages/service/shopify-migration-service/`.

### First 10 seconds

- **H1:** "Shopify migration without losing what already works." Sub: "Magento, WooCommerce, BigCommerce or something built in-house. We move the store, the data and the traffic, then watch it settle."
- Stat: "1M+ Customer profiles migrated, 13 years of history moved".
- **What she gets:** this is about exactly my fear, and they've moved more history than I have. Strong. This is the best-matched landing of the four.
- **What she doesn't get:** who they are, whether "13 years of history" is one client or many (it's one, the UK gifting brand, and it's an in-house platform, not Magento), or the cost.

### Section by section

| Section | Question | Answered? |
|---|---|---|
| "Magento to Shopify — Magento 1 and 2 stores moved without carrying the maintenance bill over." / "WooCommerce to Shopify" | "You've done Magento 1 specifically?" | Claimed, with no M1 example. The named migrations are the UK gifting brand (custom platform), Prime EVA (platform not stated) and Gunia ("Replatformed", platform not stated). |
| "Keeping Traffic — Redirects, canonicals and hreflang set so rankings survive the switch." | "Hreflang. They know multi-language." | **Yes.** Hreflang is a strong signal for an EU multi-language brand. |
| Recent Projects: "UK gifting brand — Moved off a custom platform with 1M+ customer profiles intact." | "What happened to their traffic? Any numbers?" | **No SEO outcome anywhere.** The migration page's promise is "Migrations that keep the traffic", but there is no traffic figure for a single project. That's the one number she came for. |
| "When We Can Help": "Your platform is end of life" | "That's M1." | Headline only, no body. |
| Stage 1–4, "Audit & Inventory… Launch & Watch" / "monitor Search Console for weeks after, not days" | "What do I get at the audit? A URL map spreadsheet? Is the audit paid? Subscriptions? Reviews?" | **Missing the #1 migration gotcha:** customer passwords can't be migrated to Shopify, so customers need account activation emails. ### Pains named vs missed

- **Named:** SEO loss, redirects, data integrity, custom platforms, integrations not transferring, live-during-migration, post-launch monitoring.
- **Missed:**
  - Customer password/account activation.
  - Delta sync at cutover.
  - Rollback plan ("what if we have to go back?").
  - Multi-store consolidation (M1 + Woo into one Shopify with Markets?).
  - EU-specific concerns: GDPR consent carry-over, VAT/OSS setup, EU payment methods (iDEAL, Klarna, SEPA), Shopify Markets for languages.
  - Cost range.
  - Data security during transfer: who holds the 10-year customer dump, where it's stored, and when it's deleted. As an ops manager at an EU brand she needs a DPA before handing over PII.

### Trust and distrust

- **Trust:** the tone ("Migrations rarely fail on launch day. They fail three weeks later, when nobody is looking at the coverage report."). This is the sentence that makes her believe they've done it, and it's the best line on the site.
- **Distrust:**
  - The one big proof is anonymous, and it's not her platform.
  - No SEO numbers.
  - No mention of GDPR for the data she'd hand over.
  - The blog has a whole guide ("How to Migrate from Magento to Shopify: A Practical 2026 Guide") that **the migration page doesn't link to**. She'd find it only via "Case Studies" in the nav, which promises case studies and delivers a blog.

### Drop-off

She doesn't drop off on the page itself; she's persuaded by the thinking. She stalls because there's nothing she can take to her boss: no case with numbers, no price range, no audit offer with a fixed price, and no company details for procurement. She bookmarks it and moves on to an agency with a "Free migration audit" CTA.

### Form?

**Yes, likely** (roughly 60%). This page converts best. She'd write:

> "Magento 1.9 store (~8k SKUs, 180k customers, 10 years of orders) + WooCommerce store for [market]. Moving both to Shopify, 3 languages. Priority: keep rankings and order history, no downtime. Need a quote for audit + migration and info on how you handle customer data (GDPR/DPA)."

- **Annoying fields:**
  - "Service required" has **no "Migration" option** on the migration page. She'd pick "Shopify Development" and wonder whether it will reach the right person.
  - The budget ceiling "$25k+" is too coarse, and it's in USD.

### What she'd need

- A migration case with an SEO chart (traffic 90 days before and after) and the platform named.
- A fixed-price audit offer ("Migration audit: URL inventory, data map, risk list — €X, 2 weeks, credited to the project").
- An FAQ on passwords, delta sync and rollback.
- A link to the Magento guide.
- A company entity and a line on data processing.

### Versus a strong competitor

Migration specialists (and Shopify's own migration partners) usually show:

- a checklist PDF
- SEO case charts
- "X migrations, Y% traffic retained on average"
- a fixed-price audit

Base X has the best *explanation*, and the least *evidence*.
