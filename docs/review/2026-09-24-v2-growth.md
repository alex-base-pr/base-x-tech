# Growth review v2: blog, site scaling, channels, roadmap (2026-09-24)

Question from Alex: after the relaunch, how do we improve the site, get more client leads, and should the blog get
more weight?

Inputs: dev.base-xtech.com (repo `content/pages/*.json`), the discovery spec, the four reviews dated 2026-09-24,
all 21 posts in `https://base-xtech.com/blog/sitemap-posts.xml` (fetched and parsed 2026-09-24), the public
profiles (Clutch, Shopify Partner Directory, DesignRush; Upwork, GoodFirms and Sortlist returned 403 to the
fetcher), and web searches for the core queries.

This review assumes the conversion fixes in [summary](2026-09-24-summary.md) come first: form failure state,
EUR budget, the "Working with us" block, and the $2200 card. **More traffic into a form that shows success when it
fails is wasted money.** Everything below builds on that.

---

## 0. Verdict

1. **Yes, give the blog more weight, but not more volume.** You already publish a lot: 21 posts since late May,
   including 10 in July. The problems are what gets published and how it connects to the site:
   - 7 of 21 posts are generic ecommerce, AI-opinion or seasonal topics. None of them supports a service you sell
     to your target buyer.
   - The strongest posts (4 migration guides, 5 case studies) are barely linked from the service pages.
   - Every blog CTA is a `mailto:`. Blog leads skip ClickUp and GA4, so you can't see what the blog produces.
2. **Shift the mix to proof.** Aim for about 60% case studies and project write-ups and about 40% system-specific
   integration and migration guides. The opinion and "why ecommerce needs X" posts are done. Prime EVA shows up on
   5 service pages and has no case study. Fresh Market and the Xentral work, which carry the DACH page, have none
   either. Those are the next three posts.
3. **Scale with system-specific pages, not generic programmatic pages.** Build one page per system you have
   actually delivered (Shopify + Xentral, Shopify POS + inventory), one per migration source including
   **Shopware**, and a DE twin for each DACH-relevant page. Don't build industry pages or "Shopify + every ERP"
   pages without a project behind them. The DACH page already lists 14 systems as delivered, and it can't back
   that up.
4. **Off-site work is under-used and cheap to fix.** Clutch has had no new review in 10 months and lists a
   $25–49/h rate. The Shopify Partner Directory profile says London, English only, 2 reviews. No Xentral partner
   listing exists. Karl is the highest-leverage channel you have.

### 2.2 Case study format

Current posts are narrative. Make every case study skimmable and citable:

1. **Header facts box:** client (named or described: country, category, size), systems before → after, team
   (roles, not headcount inflation), duration, year, your role (design / build / integrate / maintain).
2. **The problem in the client's numbers** (orders/day, SKUs, stores, manual hours).
3. **Architecture diagram** with labelled systems (the same SVG style as the DACH page).
4. **What we built**, split into the service capabilities, each linked to its service page.
5. **What went wrong and how we handled it.** This is the most-read and most-cited part, and it proves honesty,
   which is on-brand.
6. **Result with a metric and a date** ("stock mismatches from ~40/week to 0 in the 8 weeks after launch").
   Only numbers you have on file (see the summary's UWG note).
7. **Named quote:** person, role, country. Link to their Clutch review if one exists.
8. **"Similar project?" CTA card** → service page with the form open. Related: 1 guide + 1 case.

Schema: `Article` with `about` → the `Service` of the matching page. Add `mentions` for the named systems.
