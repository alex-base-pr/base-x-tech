- **Owner decisions respected**:
  - "AI Automation" stays.
  - No team photos for now.
  - Figma images are approved.

## Persona 1: Katrin, Head of E-Commerce, German mid-size retailer (Xentral)

**Entry:** Google "Shopify Xentral Anbindung" → `https://dev.base-xtech.com/de/pages/complex-solutions/` (desktop).

**Path:**
1. `/de/pages/complex-solutions/`, H1 "Komplexe Systeme, so verbunden, wie Ihr Unternehmen arbeitet."
2. System strip (Xentral is second) → "Expertise vor Ort" (Alexander Karl, grey placeholder) → Fresh Market case → "Tiefer einsteigen" blog cards.
3. Blog card "Nova-Poshta-Integration für Shopify…", which goes to `/blog/nova-poshta-shopify-integration-connect-app/` (an English article on prod).
4. Footer, looking for an Impressum: only "Terms & Conditions" and "Privacy policy" → `/pages/terms-and-conditions/`: "subject to the laws of Ukraine."
5. Back, then "Gespräch vereinbaren" → English modal.

**What convinces**
- The DE copy is still the most precise she will read: "Feldzuordnung und Datenhoheit… Webhooks und API-Limits… Wiederholungsversuche, Datenabgleich und Monitoring".
- The honest line "Integrierbar (noch kein abgeschlossenes Projekt): SAP Business One · Akeneo · Salesforce".
- The FAQ "Indem wir festlegen, welches System für den Bestand führend ist".
- The form now offers "Complex Solutions (ERP, POS, middleware)" and phone is optional, so she could send a written enquiry without being called.

**What blocks**
1. **No Impressum.** Still disqualifying on its own for a German B2B buyer.
2. **"Expertise vor Ort" with a grey box.** The accessible label is literally "Foto von Alexander Karl (Platzhalter)". There is no city, LinkedIn or email. "Vor Ort" promises something the page cannot show.
3. **Fresh Market has no numbers and no systems named, and its image is a placeholder too** ("Projektbild (Platzhalter)"). Two placeholders on one sales page.
4. **FAQ still dodges:** "Mit welchen ERP- und CRM-Systemen haben Sie bereits gearbeitet?" is answered with "aus den gängigen Kategorien", right under a strip that names 14 systems.
5. **The modal is English and in USD**, and it opens with "Shopify Development" and "$2K–5K" preselected. That is the wrong service and, for her, a budget four to five times too low.
6. No DSGVO/AVV line, no EU hosting statement, no contracting entity.
7. Ukraine is found out through the Nova Poshta blog card, not stated.

**Questions left unanswered:** Is Xentral a delivered project, and which direction does data flow? Who is the contracting party? Is there an AVV? Is Karl employed, a reseller or a freelancer, and does he join calls in German? What does Phase 1 cost, and what do I get at the end of it?

**Verdict:** **No (about 15%)**, up from "No (maybe if referred)" (about 10%). The form fixes help at the margin, but none of her three trust checks (Impressum, a real local contact, a named Xentral reference) has moved.

## Persona 2: Tom, founder, UK DTC brand (£2M), configurator plus B2B, burned by a freelancer

**Entry:** Google "Shopify product configurator agency UK" → `https://dev.base-xtech.com/pages/shopify-development/` (desktop).

**Path:**
1. `/pages/shopify-development/`, H1 "Shopify development for stores that need more than a theme".
2. Capabilities (Configurators, B2B & Wholesale) → Recent Projects: Prime EVA "10M+ possible configurations", which leaves to `https://primeeva.com/`.
3. Back → FAQ "Can you build a product configurator on Shopify?": "Yes, and we have… Prime EVA is the example".
4. Nav "Portfolio" → `/#section-works` on the home page. There he finds NAYA.TECH ("CES Innovation Awards Honoree, $3M+ raised… product configurator"), which links to `/blog/shopify-product-configurator-naya-tech/` (read on prod: build of about 3 months, A/B variants at 12.4% / 8.9% / 6.7% conversion).
5. Nav "About" → `/#section-team`: "Approach… Depth over shortcuts", plus badges. No people.
6. "Discuss Your Project".

**What convinces**
- **The "$2200… Ukraine" pricing card is gone.** The page no longer prices him back into freelancer territory. This was his drop-off point last round.
- The FAQ tone still speaks to his scar tissue: "Handing over a login and disappearing is where most of the problems we get called in to fix started"; "we will say so rather than sell you the upgrade".
- The Prime EVA FAQ answer gives the configurator claim a named example.
- If he reaches NAYA (via home), he finds a configurator case with real numbers and a build time. That is the proof he wanted, but it isn't on the page he landed on.

**What blocks**
1. **Who writes the code?** There are now zero people on the page, and "About" shows principles, not a team. For a buyer burned by a disappearing freelancer this is the biggest open question.
2. **B2B proof** is still "We have built this, though that client's work is not public."
3. **The headline stat is still "4.8* Upwork Reviews"**, the marketplace where he found the freelancer. The footnote now makes the Upwork source even more visible.
4. **No price anchor at all.** He can't tell whether they are £10k or £60k. The form forces a bucket, with "$2K–5K" preselected, and he fears picking low gets him a junior.
5. The NAYA configurator case is **not linked from Shopify Development**. Prime EVA sends him off-site.
6. "When We Can Help" cards ("One store must serve wholesale and retail") still have no body.

**Questions left unanswered:** Who is the lead developer, and can I talk to them before signing? How do we work week to week (staging, demos, milestone payments)? Who owns the repo? This FAQ exists on Apps but not here. What does a configurator plus B2B build typically cost?

**Verdict:** **Maybe (about 55%)**, up from about 40%. Removing the pricing card took away the reason he bounced. What's missing now is a face or name, and a price range.

## Persona 5 (new): Dana, US DTC founder, $3M GMV, redesign plus CRO, compares 3 agencies on her phone in one evening

**Profile:** Apparel/lifestyle DTC on Shopify, $3M GMV, conversion rate flat. Wants a redesign that lifts conversion, not just a prettier store. She has three tabs open, from a Clutch shortlist and a founder-Slack recommendation. She gives each agency about 5 minutes and asks each tab: can they show me stores like mine, what did it do to conversion, roughly what does it cost, who will I work with, and can I book a call tonight?

**Entry:** Clutch listing / Google "Shopify redesign agency CRO" → `https://dev.base-xtech.com/pages/service/custom-web-design-services/` at 375px.

**Path (375×812):**
1. `/pages/service/custom-web-design-services/`. First screen: the "Web Design" chip, then **about 350px of empty black**, and the H1 "Shopify design for products that take more than one click" begins at y≈420. On her first screen she sees half a headline and no work.
2. Scrolls: Capabilities (/01 UX/UI Design … /06 Creative Direction) → Recent Projects: Innan ("Brand-led Shopify storefront for a jewellery label") → Prime EVA (car mats) → Gunia (Ukrainian homeware).
3. Taps Innan → `/blog/innan-jewellery-shopify-case-study/` (prod). It's a well-written Berlin atelier story, but **no conversion or revenue number** appears.
4. Back → "Client Stories": "Stealth Startup US — Their team delivered a bug-free Shopify store that resulted in higher conversion rates."
5. FAQ "What does Shopify store design cost?": "It depends on the number of page types… We scope every project before quoting". No range.
6. Menu → Conversion Rate Optimization → `/pages/service/conversion-rate-optimization/`. The stat "50-200% conversion uplift range" has no case attached. The project cards are the same three brands.
7. Menu → Portfolio → `/#section-works`: **NAYA.TECH, "CES Innovation Awards Honoree, $3M+ raised"**. This is the first thing that looks like her world (a US DTC hardware brand). The case on prod has an A/B test (12.4% vs 6.7% conversion). But she got there by accident, three taps deep.
8. Header "Let's talk" → modal: name, email, phone (optional), company, service ("Shopify Development" preselected), budget ("$2K–5K" preselected), message. No calendar link, no phone number.

Web Design is about 10,300px tall at 375px (about 13 screens). CRO is about 11,600px. A 5-minute comparison reads the first 3–4 screens and the FAQ.

**What convinces**
- **The mobile build is good.** Loads fast, no horizontal scroll, sticky "Let's talk", one-tap modal, a big single-column form, "Phone (optional)", USD buckets that fit a US buyer.
- **"We audit, prioritise and then actually build the changes. Not a slide deck of recommendations"** (CRO H1 sub). That is exactly her frustration with CRO consultancies.
- "The same team that finds the problem builds the fix."
- "Do we need enough traffic for A/B testing?" is an honest answer, which makes the agency feel senior.
- The home badges (Upwork Top Rated, Clutch Global, DesignRush) are recognisable to a US founder.

**What blocks**
1. **No before/after and no conversion result on the two pages she came for.** The only real CRO number on the whole site (NAYA A/B test) is on a prod blog post, linked only from the home page. The "50-200% conversion uplift range" with no source reads as a made-up claim next to competitors showing "+23% CVR for [brand]".
2. **No portfolio that looks like her.** Jewellery (Berlin atelier), car mats and Ukrainian homeware. NAYA, the one US DTC brand, isn't on Web Design or CRO.
3. **No price signal.** Both FAQs defer to scoping. Her other two tabs show "Redesigns from $25k" or a CRO retainer price, so this tab loses the "can I afford them?" test by default.
4. **Anonymous reviews.** "Stealth Startup US", "Lili Mager", "Gunia". She can't tell whether any of them is a DTC founder like her.
5. **No booking.** No Calendly or phone, only a form that replies "by email shortly". The other agency lets her pick a slot tonight.
6. **Who and where?** No team, no location, no hours. She won't find out that Kyiv is UTC+3 (about 7 hours ahead of New York), which overlaps US mornings. That is fine for her, but unstated, and she would rather be told than surprised on the first call.
7. **First screen at 375px is mostly empty** on every service page (the H1 starts around y≈420–435). Her first impression is black space, not work.

**Questions left unanswered:** What did your last redesign do to conversion? What does a redesign plus 3 months of CRO cost? Who is my designer, and my point of contact? Can we talk this week in my time zone?

**Verdict:** **Maybe (about 30%).** The site passes the mobile quality test and the CRO positioning is right. It fails the three things a one-evening comparison runs on: a result number, a price range and a face or booking link. She would shortlist it only if a referral vouched for them.

## Top 8 cross-persona blockers, with fixes

Ranked by how many personas hit it × how badly. "Bridge" means copy, config or small template work that fits before launch.

### 1. The best proof is not on the page where the buyer decides (5/5)
- **What's wrong:**
  - NAYA.TECH (a US brand with an A/B test of 12.4% vs 6.7%, a build of about 3 months, CES Honoree) is only on the home page.
  - The UK gifting and Innan cases sit on the blog.
  - Service pages carry three anonymous reviews ("VT Advantec / Lili Mager / Gunia", "Stealth Startup US") and "4.8* Upwork" as the headline stat.
  - CRO shows "50-200% conversion uplift range" with no source.
- **Fix (bridge):**
  - Add the NAYA card to **Shopify Development, Web Design and CRO**. On CRO, put the real number on the card ("A/B test: 12.4% vs 6.7% conversion").
  - Replace "50-200%" with that sourced figure, or remove it (STATUS already notes it "needs source on file").
  - Add role and country to every review ("Operations Director, VT Advantec, Germany") and link each to its Clutch/Upwork source.
  - Put the Clutch badge next to the Upwork stat on service pages, not only on the home page.
  - Add one sentence of numbers to Fresh Market (locations, sync interval, oversells before and after).

### 2. Nobody is visible and nothing says where the company is (5/5)
- **What's wrong:**
  - Service pages now name no one.
  - "About" → `/#section-team` shows an "Approach" block and badges.
  - "Kyiv" appears nowhere.
  - Karl is a grey box labelled "(Platzhalter)".
- **Fix (bridge, respecting "no team photos for now"):**
  - Add a text-only "Who you'll work with" strip to each service page: name, role, years, LinkedIn link for the tech lead, the PM and the design lead. No photos needed.
  - Add one line under the stats block: "Engineering team in Kyiv, Ukraine (UTC+3). Working hours overlap CET fully and US Eastern mornings. Clients in the UK, DACH, EU and US since 20XX."
  - Point "About" at that strip until the About page is written.
  - **Karl until his photo arrives:** replace the grey box with a monogram or initials tile, and add his city, LinkedIn and a direct German-language email. Remove "(Platzhalter)" from the aria-label. Change "Expertise vor Ort" to "Ansprechpartner für die DACH-Region" unless he can actually meet on site.

### 3. The contact modal defaults to the wrong service and the lowest budget on every page (5/5)
- **What's wrong:**
  - "Shopify Development" and "$2K–5K" are preselected on Migration, Apps, DE Complex and CRO.
  - Leads will be misrouted and under-sized, and buyers read the default as the agency's typical ticket.
  - There is no partnership option.
  - The modal is USD-only and English on `/de/`.
- **Fix (bridge):**
  - Preselect the service from the page the modal was opened on (a `data-service` on each trigger).
  - Leave the budget **unselected** (or default to "Not sure").
  - Add "Agency / white-label partnership", with the budget hidden for it.
  - On `/de/` and `/pages/complex-solutions/`, show EUR buckets ("< €10k / €10–25k / €25–50k / €50k+ / Noch offen") and localise the modal labels and success text.
  - Add a "Book a 30-min call" link (Calendly or similar) beside the submit button for buyers like Dana who want a slot tonight.

### 4. There is no price anchor anywhere now (4/5: Tom, Dana, Lukas, Katrin)
- **What's wrong:** removing the "$2200" card was right, but every "What does it cost?" answer now defers to scoping. Buyers comparing tabs drop the one they can't place.
- **Fix (bridge):** add one "Typical engagements" line per service, as ranges, in the page's voice. For example:
  - Web Design: "Storefront redesign, design + build: typically $X–Y, 6–12 weeks."
  - CRO: "Audit + first sprint from $X; ongoing from $Y/month."
  - Integrations and Complex: "Paid discovery €X, credited to the build."
  - Apps, for agencies: "Day rate on request, EUR/CHF."
  - Ranges are enough. The numbers are the owner's call.

### 5. DACH and EU legal and data basics are missing (3/5: Katrin fully, Marta, Lukas)
- **What's wrong:**
  - No Impressum (404 on all three likely URLs).
  - The privacy policy still says "By using our Website, you agree to the collection…", lists Google Ads remarketing and Hotjar, and names no controller or address.
  - The terms say "subject to the laws of Ukraine".
  - No line on DPA/AVV for client data passing through integrations or migrations.
- **Fix (bridge):**
  - Publish a Legal notice / Impressum (EN + DE) with the legal entity and address, and link it in the footer.
  - Add one FAQ to Migration, Integrations and DE Complex: "How do you handle our customer data? DPA/AVV under Art. 28 GDPR, processing location, deletion after the project."
  - Fix the consent wording in the privacy policy before prod analytics go live.

### 6. FAQs still skip the sharpest buyer-specific question (4/5)
- **What's wrong:**
  - **Katrin:** the ERP answer says "aus den gängigen Kategorien" right under a strip of 14 named systems.
  - **Marta:** nothing on passwords, cutover delta or rollback.
  - **Tom:** nothing on how you work week to week, milestones or repo ownership on the Dev page.
  - **Dana:** the cost answer dodges (see blocker 4).
- **Fix (bridge):**
  - Name the delivered systems in the ERP answer, mirroring the honest "noch kein abgeschlossenes Projekt" line.
  - Add three migration FAQs:
    - "Will customers need new passwords?" (yes, activation invites and the rollout plan),
    - "What about orders placed during cutover?" (delta sync),
    - "Can we roll back?".
  - Add "How will we work together?" to the Dev and Design pages: staging, weekly demo, milestone payments, and "the repo is yours".

### 7. Page sections stop short: empty "When We Can Help" cards and stages with no deliverables (5/5, cheap to fix)
- **What's wrong:**
  - All six EN service pages show three headline-only cards ("Stock is wrong by the time it syncs", "Your platform is end of life", "Buyers can't tell your options apart").
  - Stages 01–04 describe activities, not output, duration or whether step 1 is paid.
- **Fix (bridge):**
  - Add a two-line body to each card. The DE Complex page already has the pattern.
  - Add a "You get / takes" line per stage, for example: "01 Audit & Inventory · 1–2 weeks · you get: URL map, data map, risk list and a fixed quote for 02–04."
