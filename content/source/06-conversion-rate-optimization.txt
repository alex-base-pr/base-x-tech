FAQ
faq.h2 → Questions we get asked

Answers must render in the HTML whether a row is open or closed. Confirm with Dima.
What does a CRO audit include?
We go through the whole funnel: homepage, listing and product pages, cart, checkout, mobile, speed, and whatever your analytics is or is not recording. Every problem comes back grouped by area and scored on impact, effort and confidence, so you get an ordered list rather than observations.
Do you implement the changes or just recommend them?
We implement them. That is the difference between this and an audit you then have to find a developer for. The same team that finds the problem builds the fix, which is also why we can tell you honestly which fixes are cheap and which are not.
How do you decide what to fix first?
Every hypothesis gets scored on impact, effort and confidence, then ordered. Quick wins go first because they fund the argument for the bigger work. You approve the scope before anything gets built, so the order of work is agreed rather than assumed.
Do we need enough traffic for A/B testing?
For testing, yes. Below a certain volume a test cannot reach significance, and running one anyway just delays the fix. On lower-traffic stores we implement the changes we are confident about and measure the funnel rather than the variant.
How long before we see results?
Quick wins show inside the first reporting cycle. Structural work on product pages or checkout takes longer to read, because you need enough sessions to separate the change from normal variation. We set what success looks like before starting.
Can you fix site speed as part of this?
Yes, and it is usually on the list. Images, third-party scripts, unused apps and theme code all slow a store down, and speed affects mobile conversion most. It is development work, which is why it sits with us rather than a separate specialist.
What analytics do we need in place?
Working GA4 ecommerce events at minimum, and a session recording tool if you want to see behaviour rather than infer it. If tracking is broken or missing we fix that first, because measuring an improvement against bad data tells you nothing.
Is this a one-off project or ongoing?
Both work, and ongoing works better. A single round finds and fixes the obvious problems. After that CRO is a cycle: implement, measure, update the backlog, pick the next thing. Stores that treat it as a project stop improving the moment it ends.

The last answer is the commercial one. It makes the retainer case in the buyer's own terms rather than as a pricing model, which is the LTV argument you made when you split this page out.
